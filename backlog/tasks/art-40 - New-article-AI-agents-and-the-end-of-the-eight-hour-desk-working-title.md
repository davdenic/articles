**Draft (as provided by David, 2026-10-18):**

> Do we still need to sit at a desk for eight hours to get our work done?
>
> For decades, our working environment has been built around a simple assumption: to work, we need to be at our workstation.
>
> Large desks. Desktop computers. Fixed places. Fixed working hours.
>
> But our tools have changed.
>
> We carry powerful computers in our backpacks. Our documents are in the cloud. And with AI agents, some tasks can continue running even when we are not actively working on them.
>
> I can start a coding task, go to the gym, and check its progress from my phone. I can launch a research task and review the results later. I can let an agent work while I spend time with my family.
>
> Of course, AI still needs supervision. Its output needs to be checked, and some tasks require our direct involvement.
>
> But this changes the relationship between our time and our work.
>
> Instead of spending hours waiting for tasks to finish, perhaps we can organise our work around moments of focused attention, decisions and reviews.
>
> The same question applies to our offices. Do we still need to organise every room around a permanent desk when our tools and workflows have become so much more flexible?
>
> Technology changes more than the tools we use. It challenges the assumptions behind the way we work.
>
> Perhaps it's time to rethink both.

**Expansion ideas (not binding):** supervision costs and failure modes of unattended agents; the gym example as a concrete anecdote; office implications (hot-desking, meeting rooms vs focus rooms); what still genuinely needs presence; tie-in to async-first workflows; open questions to keep the curious voice.
