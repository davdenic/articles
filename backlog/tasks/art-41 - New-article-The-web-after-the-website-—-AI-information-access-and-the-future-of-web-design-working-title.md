## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
**What:** Write a new article expanding the pasted draft: "The web after the website" — how AI changes information consumption, whether the website dies or evolves into an interface-free knowledge source, and what that means for web design disciplines (UX/UI/IA) built over 30 years. Include the meta-question: why build a website at all when a chatbot can answer — and could the site itself become a curated collection of md/transcripts/resources that feeds AI systems (llms.txt, structured content, APIs).

**Why:** Pairs with the 0005 AI/GDPR article and the eight-hour-desk piece (ART-40): a trilogy on AI reshaping assumptions — privacy, work, and now the web itself. The draft is strong on reflection; it needs the sharper angles David identified (below) to avoid being a generic "AI changes everything" essay.

**Draft:** Full draft provided by David 2026-10-18 (title: "The web after the website: how AI is changing the way we consume information"). Two passes included; second pass adds the design-disciplines tension and closing direction. Treat the later Italian notes as the editorial spine.

**Expansion spine (from David's notes, binding):**
1. Site as interface, not destination — central thesis.
2. Design doesn't disappear, its object changes: from static pages to interactions, contextual responses, actions.
3. Sustainability of information: if AI answers without driving traffic, who funds original content? Zero-click web economics.
4. The paradox: decades spent making people better at using interfaces, now building systems that remove the need for them. Which UX principles are universal vs. tied to the medium? (Cognitive-load findings vs. historical/commercial conventions.)
5. People may not want to stop reading — they just may not want to read everything when they need one answer. Long-form, docs, and assistants coexist with different purposes.
6. Concrete meta-question to land: can a website be a curated collection of md, transcripts, and resources designed to feed AI — making the site a knowledge base with many consumers, not a page for humans only.
7. Tone: open reflection, no "the web is dead" proclamations; evolution already in progress, not a forecast.
